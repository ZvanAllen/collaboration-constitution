# Project management covenant

This companion translates the Collaboration Constitution into defaults for planning, delivery, and project oversight. It supplements rather than replaces a team's established project-management system.

## Decision order

When delivery pressures conflict with the constitution:

1. Protect people, participants, collaborators, and confidential information.
2. Preserve human responsibility for decisions, interpretation, commitments, and status claims.
3. Preserve traceability from source evidence to decisions and actions.
4. Make uncertainty, disagreement, dependencies, and constraints visible.
5. Optimize schedule, scope, cost, and convenience only after the above are addressed.

## Sources of truth

Each project should identify canonical records for:

- current objective and status
- decisions and rationale
- actions, owners, status, and due dates
- milestones and dependencies
- risks and open questions
- source records and supporting evidence

AI memory, chat history, and generated summaries are not canonical records unless they are reviewed and promoted into the designated project records.

## Human responsibility

Humans remain responsible for:

- approving scope and priority
- accepting decisions and tradeoffs
- assigning or accepting ownership
- committing to deadlines
- declaring project status
- communicating externally
- closing risks, actions, or projects

AI may extract, reconcile, draft, check, and propose. It must not invent commitments, owners, dates, decisions, or progress.

## Evidence and uncertainty

Project claims should be traceable to the lightest adequate evidence:

- source meeting record for a decision or commitment
- approved plan for scope and milestones
- project register for current action status
- dated note for an assumption or judgement call
- verification result for a completion claim

Use `To confirm` or an equivalent explicit marker when evidence is incomplete or conflicting.

## Actions and decisions

An actionable commitment should record:

- stable identifier
- concise description
- accountable owner, when confirmed
- due date, when stated
- allowed status
- source date and record
- completion or cancellation date, when applicable

Decisions should be recorded separately from tentative ideas. Material changes should preserve the previous state or amendment history rather than silently replacing it.

## Status reporting

Status summaries should distinguish:

- confirmed progress
- planned work
- blocked or waiting work
- risks and dependencies
- unresolved questions
- information not recently verified

AI-generated status reports require human review before circulation when they affect expectations, accountability, funding, staffing, or external partners.

## Consent boundaries

AI may proceed with local and reversible preparation, including draft plans, summaries, registers, and checks.

Ask before:

- assigning work to a person
- changing an agreed deadline or status
- publishing or sending a status report
- contacting stakeholders
- closing or cancelling actions
- altering shared project systems
- exposing identifiable or confidential content

## Review cadence

Teams should periodically review:

- stale actions and overdue milestones
- decisions lacking traceable evidence
- unresolved `To confirm` items
- risks without owners or mitigations
- AI-generated records not yet reviewed
- whether the workflow is serving collaborators equitably and accessibly
